How does the sea reveal its condition to us? Glowing Traces brings the state of the fjord to life through the growth of phytoplankton—microscopic algae that respond to nutrients, light, salinity, and temperature—in the form of a fascinating, interactive installation. Inspired by spectacular satellite images of ocean discoloration caused by algal blooms, the project translates real-time data from the fjord into a dynamic visualization: temperature and salinity directly shape the size and extent of the phytoplankton structures.

By projecting onto the surface of water in a round basin, the installation creates a tangible connection to the real ocean. The round shape is reminiscent of a Petri dish and underscores the scientific perspective on the ecosystem. A live counter displays the continuous flow of data and its immediate effects in the “digital ocean.” The visualization was implemented using cables.gl.

Glowing Traces was created together with Katharina Fedde in the “Interactive Information Design” master’s program. The project was developed in collaboration with Prof. Dr. Stephan Juricke and Prof. Dr. Florian Schütte from GEOMAR Helmholtz Center for Ocean Research Kiel.
